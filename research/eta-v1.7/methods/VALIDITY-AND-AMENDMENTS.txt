Validity threats and protocol history
This disclosure accompanies ETA Preprint v1.7 editorial r2. It retains favorable
and inconclusive evidence. Source hashes appear in PROVENANCE.json.

INPUT CONTENT AND CONSTRUCT VALIDITY
F's 40 validation first-result commands are initial read-only orientation/search.
38 results contain an explicit rg-not-found error, despite an outer exit code
of zero; a pipeline's final exit status does not certify every command succeeded.
Normal anchors occur at 4.999-9.003 seconds; delayed anchors at 14.999-19.002.
They are limited examples of rich task progress. These observations do not mean
the tools never executed or that 38 solver tasks failed. All remain in analysis.

The previous private input audit detected identical inner/outer result wrappers
in all 33 E Full anchor inputs and all 80 F Full anchor inputs. Corresponding
Activity lifecycle observations also contain repetition. This is representation
duplication, not duplicated model dispatch or double scoring. Its bias direction
is unknown. Old predictions cannot be called deduplicated predictions after an
input edit. G instead makes fresh requests with canonicalized inputs.

Legacy packages change semantic content, length, roles, flags, assistant text
and derived fields together. F information-arm labels are visible. G matches
more fields, but content length still varies and the prompt remains the archived
general ETA prompt. No study here isolates individual contributions of successful
tests, failed tests, edits, source reads or execution semantics independent of length.

G's input contract and byte-prefix checks restrict future visibility. The release
does not publish all original text or independently prove semantic non-leakage.
Targeted checks cannot observe provider internals or eliminate all implicit cues.
The actual prompt/schema and all structural blocks make the implementation more
inspectable while this content-level reproducibility gap remains explicit.

ORIGINAL DESIGN AND AMENDMENTS
F originally freezes 28 tasks (8 pilot +20 validation), paired normal/delayed
runs, at most 56 solver starts, 80 pilot-normal ETA and 400 validation ETA.
Pilot delay is 30 seconds after an actually executed result. Seven complete
pilot pairs determine a 10-second validation dose; six eligible normal pilot
tasks determine q and three truth factors. Validation does not enter those fits.

The original primary tests Full versus frozen q*Activity in the delayed
environment and its paired interaction relative to normal, with repository
bootstrap seed 20261022. The original delayed difference is -171.4 seconds,
95% interval [-378.1,20.2]; the interaction is +98.7 [-209.2,391.1]. The joint
benefit criterion is not met. Later raw or P60 analyses do not replace this test.

Output handling: pilot pair 4 interrupts at the old 2 MiB buffer. Both starts
are retained, without completion times or known final usage and without retries.
Subsequent runs in both environments use stream capture; combined output <=2 MiB
is returned unchanged, while above that threshold pipes longer than 128 KiB
show their first/last 64 KiB with an explicit truncation notice. Private archived
hidden output is not substituted into ETA inputs. Earlier successful pilot
traces retain the old profile. This changes collection behavior and may add I/O.

Scheduling: the first 14 validation pairs run serially. The last six use an
authorized two-solver paired-wave amendment; five actually overlap and one
recovers serially. ETA requests remain serial and do not overlap active solver
runs. Storage capacity changes from 751619276800 to 1073741824000 bytes before
the first amended pair (700 to 1000 GiB; the paper uses the shorthand GB).
These nonrandom small strata cannot identify a concurrency effect independently
of storage, time, workload or other operational changes. Preserve both planned
schedule and actual overlap. No claim of serial/parallel equivalence is made.

F also retains the dependency-amended validation task, output truncation and
the serial-recovery observation gap in its limitations/sensitivities. Removing
an affected case is a sensitivity analysis and does not replace the full cohort
because it yields a more favorable interval. Delayed feedback may change the
solver's path: all 20 pairs change executed-tool counts. They are stochastic
paired executions, not one trajectory with a mechanically stretched clock.

G preparation: rejected tool arguments were retained explicitly before the input
freeze. Initial coordinator ownership failed before any worker/model started.
It was repaired under the existing identity requirement. The first real request
then completed; a JSON key-order identity alarm was resolved by structural
equality. That completed request was counted once and never repeated. All
original recovery records are retained privately and their hashes are listed.

DEPENDENCE, SELECTION AND INFERENCE
B's four-model fixed-point study retains 608 starts and 602 numeric responses;
584 common-valid rows enter matched model comparisons. Its 19 tasks include 11
originally admitted and eight reassessed sources. A partial source stays outside
the fixed-point set. The same 16-task support is used for its cross-stage profile.
Fractional anchors require final duration and are not validated online triggers.

G reuses F normal trajectories. B bridge requests reuse B's solver trajectories;
C diagnostics reuse C development sources. E's separate timing-qualified sources
provide a different kind of evidence, but legacy packaging limits interpretation.
Do not add calls, repeats, environments, anchors or studies into an independent N.

The raw presentation was chosen after viewing results. Many unadjusted intervals
are reported. Small repository counts, especially eight G evaluation and four
calibration repositories, limit stability. A zero-crossing interval is neither
proof of no effect nor equivalence. Retain raw and original calibrated findings.
Reduced overestimation can improve MAE without improving ranking: all G P60
forecasts overestimate, so its raw MAE gain entirely reduces overestimation.
Every G paired Pearson-difference interval crosses zero.

ACCOUNTING AND AUDIT COVERAGE
F: 56 closed starts, 54 completed timing sources, two interrupted pilot sources
with unknown final usage; 69 pilot and 400 validation numeric ETA responses,
plus a separately retained zero-model failure. Validation correctness is 14/20
normal and 13/20 delayed. Incorrect repairs are not excluded just for correctness.
G: 144 completed, known-usage, released ETA requests, including 48 calibration
and 96 evaluation, and zero new solvers. Recorded known tokens total 4,091,756;
this is not monetary cost. The local curation verifies all 144 archived calls.

The earlier E/F audit checked 532 actual requests and 19,634 assertions; B/C
analyses mainly recomputed frozen row-level evidence. This package includes
derived E/F audit metadata, not their private requests. Public hash/count tests
cannot replace transcript inspection or independent experimental replication.
The numeric supplement covers selected raw tables/figure components; delivery
integration, every correlation interval and calibrated appendices remain outside
its executable scope. Those limitations should remain in Data Availability.

FRAMEWORK AND PRACTICAL VALIDITY
G +Content is not the plugin's complete real input/transport/scheduling pipeline.
Point error, useful delivery coverage, code correctness and user benefit are
separate outcomes. No participant study establishes a user-experience benefit.
The results support conditional information/time hypotheses and an inspectable
framework, not universal Full superiority or an optimal online trigger policy.
